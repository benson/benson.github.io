how to grade a case

You are checking what the answers demonstrate—not guessing how smart the writer is, or predicting the model’s score. A case is one set of five answers.

  1. Read the five answers once. Then use the instructions beside each rating to count the reasoning that is actually present.
  2. Choose one score for each of the five dimensions. The dropdowns explain each score; “examples & scoring notes” shows how to apply it. Nothing is preselected.
  3. Add a short reason for your ratings, especially borderline calls. Save only when ready: saving locks this case’s judgment and reveals its model comparison.

These numbers are rubric points, not school grades, percentiles, or IQ. A 50 does not mean “below average”; a 100 means the listed criteria were demonstrated, not that an answer is perfect in every way.

partial credit, corrections, and when to choose “uncertain”
  • Count each criterion once, only when the text supports it. One short sentence may earn several criteria. Do not invent a missing explanation or demand particular words.
  • Partial credit comes from the criteria shown—not a general impression. Do not award half a criterion or round an updating score to a school grade.
  • Credit an explicit final correction. An earlier mistake does not cancel corrected reasoning, but do not silently repair a contradiction for the writer.
  • Use a numeric score when you can judge the evidence—even if the answer is weak or incomplete. A clearly absent criterion earns no points.
  • Choose “uncertain” when missing, unreadable, or genuinely ambiguous text prevents a reliable judgment. It is not a middle score and does not count as zero. Explain what you could not judge in the notes.
  • Ignore spelling, dialect, confidence, and fancy vocabulary. A wrong answer can still be clearly communicated; score each dimension independently.
