# why it's bad to "get good at politics"

i read a lot of posts [[How I influence tech company politics as a staff software engineer](https://www.seangoedecke.com/how-to-influence-politics/)] that describe how to operate successfully in a company that is run by "real political operators."

i am taking the opposite stance: **if you are spending your time thinking about "how to influence politics" internally, you are working at an ineffective company, and you should leave as soon as you have the option to do so.** 

when you hear someone in tech describe their dream job, they never describe a place full of extremely skilled political operators. no, what most people want is an organization where _these skills aren't the most important skills to have_. they want to work at an organization where the primary value they bring is through their engineering, or product, or design skills, not their skills in managing organizational politics. 

once you've been at a company a few years, you can tell what type of company you're at. who is being promoted? who is being successful? is it the teams and individuals with the best ideas, executed the best, or is it the teams and individuals who have the best relationships with executives? do people want to work on the thing that makes the business the most money, or do they want to work on the shiny new idea the CEO just had over the weekend? how much of your time is spent working on the plan for what to build, how to roll it out, how to communicate with users, and how much time are you working on how to pitch it internally?

this has been the complete joy of building solo. it's isolating and quieter and less fun... but 100% of the time i'm woring is devoted to making a better product. i'm not spending 1:1s discussing the best technique to convince an exec of the plan that we all agree is right, i'm actually spending that time on the plan itself (and then on the execution).
